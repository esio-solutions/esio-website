Lina runs Café Nordkraft, a 9-seat speciality coffee bar in Aalborg. Before ESIO, she described her finances in two phases: "before the bookkeeper sends the report" and "after". She would log into her bank app on Tuesdays, see a number that was different from what she expected, and spend the rest of the day quietly worried.

Three months in, she described it differently:

> *"I open ESIO before I open the till. I know what's coming, what's leaving, and what's mine. The bank balance is just a confirmation now."*

## What changed

- **Daily forecasts replaced monthly reports.** Lina no longer waits for the bookkeeper's reconciliation to know her position.
- **Tax obligations are pre-allocated.** Money owed to SKAT is tagged as such immediately. There's no longer a "where did that 14,000 kr come from?" moment in March.
- **Supplier payment timing is deliberate.** When a coffee bean shipment arrives, Lina sees the cashflow impact before authorising the payment.

She also told us the most useful feature is one we almost cut: a tiny indicator on the dashboard that shows whether *today's* expected balance is above or below safe minimum. That's the question she actually had — not "what's the forecast," but "am I OK today?"

We left the indicator in.

Lina's experience is the textbook case of [the gap between profit and cash](/articles/cashflow-vs-profit/) — books say one thing, the bank says another, and a daily indicator is what lets a founder live in the uncertainty without losing the day to it.
