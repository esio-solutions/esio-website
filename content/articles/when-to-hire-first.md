The "when do I hire?" question gets answered emotionally — usually right after a busy week. The financial answer is dull: when you can pay them for the next 12 months without strain, even if revenue stays flat.

## The math, simplified

For a Danish full-time employee earning DKK 35,000/month gross:

- Gross salary: 35,000 × 12 = **420,000**
- Pension (around 12% combined): ~**50,400**
- Holiday pay (12.5%): ~**52,500**
- ATP, AM-bidrag, social: ~**8,000**
- Workspace, equipment, tooling: ~**40,000–60,000**

**Loaded annual cost: ~570,000–590,000 DKK** for one mid-level hire.

## The 12-month rule

You can afford the hire if you can pay them, with current cash and realistic forward revenue, for 12 months. Not "if revenue grows 20%." With *current* numbers.

That sounds conservative because it is. The cost of hiring too early — having to fire, demoralised team, your own burnout — is far higher than the cost of waiting two extra months.
