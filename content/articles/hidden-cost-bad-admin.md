Most founders track the obvious costs: salaries, rent, software. The expensive ones are the costs that never appear on a P&L:

- **An invoice sent two weeks late.** That's two weeks of cash you didn't have, multiplied by every late invoice you send. Founders consistently underestimate this — a 30-day average payment time becomes 45 days, and your DSO quietly eats your runway.
- **A deduction missed.** SKAT doesn't notify you about deductions you forgot to claim. Travel, software, training, home-office percentage, depreciation — small businesses leave thousands of kroner on the table every year.
- **A duplicate subscription.** When five people each sign up for tools individually, you discover the duplicate spend at year-end. Or never.
- **A 30-minute reconciliation, every Monday.** That's one full work day per month, in perpetuity, plus the cognitive cost of context-switching back to the actual business.

## What "good admin" actually looks like

Not perfect — *boringly automatic*. You should not be the system that catches duplicates, deadlines, or missed deductions. The system should catch them and only escalate the genuine decisions to you.

That's the line we drew when we built ESIO: anything that's a recurring scan-and-flag job is the software's problem. Anything that requires judgement is yours.
