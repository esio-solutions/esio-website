If you run a small business in Denmark, your year is shaped by a small set of dates that rarely move. Miss one and SKAT will remind you, with interest. Here's the cycle, simplified.

## Quarterly: VAT (moms)

Most small businesses report VAT quarterly. The deadline is the **1st day of the third month after the quarter ends**:

- Q1 (Jan–Mar) → due **1 June**
- Q2 (Apr–Jun) → due **1 September**
- Q3 (Jul–Sep) → due **1 December**
- Q4 (Oct–Dec) → due **1 March**

If your annual turnover is below DKK 5 million, you can apply for half-yearly reporting; below DKK 1 million, yearly. Most growing businesses graduate from yearly to quarterly within their first 18 months.

## Monthly: payroll obligations

If you pay yourself a salary or have employees:

- **A-skat** (income tax withheld) — due the **10th of the following month** for businesses, **17th** for some public-sector employers.
- **AM-bidrag** (8% labour-market contribution) — same deadline as A-skat.
- **ATP**, holiday pay accruals, etc. — handled monthly via your payroll provider.

## Annual

- **B-skat** instalments (for self-employed) — 10 instalments per year, mid-month.
- **Annual report** to Erhvervsstyrelsen — depends on company class; ApS deadlines run from 5 to 6 months after fiscal year-end.
- **Final tax return** — typically May for the prior year.
