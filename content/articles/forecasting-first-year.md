The hardest forecast to build is the first one. By the time you have 18 months of trading data, you can extrapolate; in your first year, you're guessing — but some guesses are more disciplined than others.

## Build from drivers, not from totals

Don't forecast "revenue: DKK 80,000/mo". Forecast the *drivers*: customers per month, average order value, churn, sales-cycle length. Then the totals follow. This sounds like extra work, but the moment reality diverges from the plan, you can see *which driver* missed — and adjust just that one.

## Pick three scenarios, not one

A single "plan" is a single bet. Build:

1. **Realistic**: what you actually expect.
2. **Pessimistic**: half the customer count, slower payment cycles.
3. **Stretch**: what happens if one channel really works.

The pessimistic case tells you when you'd have to act. That's the more useful number.

## Cash, then revenue

If you only have time for one forecast, forecast cash. Revenue can be misleading (especially with payment delays); cash determines whether you can pay payroll on the 28th. ESIO defaults to cash because it's the question that actually keeps founders up at night.

## Revisit monthly

A first-year forecast is wrong on day 1 and slightly less wrong every month. The discipline isn't predicting accurately — it's noticing the gap between forecast and actual, and asking why.
